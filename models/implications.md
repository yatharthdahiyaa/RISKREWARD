# Implications

## What do our findings mean, and who should act on them?

Our results show that **a data scientist's success depends on different things at different career stages**: experience and skills when getting hired, maths and storytelling as a junior, and personality as a senior.
Below is what this means for each group of people.

---

## 1. Students and job seekers

| Finding | What to do |
|---|---|
| Each year of experience adds about **12%** to pay | **Start gaining experience early** through internships, live projects and freelance work. Every year counts. |
| Data Scientist earns about **2.3×** a Data Analyst at the same experience | **Aim for Data Scientist or Data Engineer roles.** Treat Data Analyst as a starting point, not a final goal. |
| Specific skills (ML, NLP, R, algorithms, statistical modelling) give **2–3×** better odds of a ₹15 L+ job | **Learn specific, in-demand technical skills.** Writing "communication skills" or "analytical" on a CV does not raise pay. |
| **67%** of jobs are at lower-paying IT-services firms | **Expect your first job to be at a big IT firm.** Use it to build experience, then move to better-paying companies. |

> **Key message:** build experience early, learn specialised skills, and aim for higher job titles.

---

## 2. Universities and training institutes

| Finding | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Maths/stats** is the strongest driver of a junior's salary hike (×6.2) | **Make statistics a core subject**, not an optional extra. |
| **Storytelling and dashboards** are the second strongest (×3.9) | **Teach students to explain data**, through presentations, dashboards and business reports. |
| Big-data tools only help *on top of* strong maths and storytelling | **Do not teach tools alone.** Build the basics first, then add tools. |
| Seniors succeed through **openness** and **conscientiousness** | **Build these qualities too**, through research projects, case studies, deadlines and teamwork. |

> **Key message:** the skills gap is in **thinking and communicating with data**, not just in coding tools.

---

## 3. HR teams and hiring managers

| Finding | What to do |
|---|---|
| Junior hikes can be predicted with **about 82–86% accuracy** from skills | **Use a clear skill-score system** for hike decisions. This makes them **fair and easy to explain** to employees. |
| A simple rule works: storytelling > 4.15 and maths > 3.55 → mostly a high hike | **Share these benchmarks with juniors** so they know exactly what to improve. |
| Senior success can be predicted with **95% accuracy** from personality | **Use personality assessments** when hiring or promoting for senior and client-facing roles. |
| The "conscientious & open" type succeeds **89%** of the time; "agreeable but not open" succeeds **0%** | **Use the personality types for coaching.** Help seniors build curiosity and ownership, not just friendliness. |
| Neuroticism alone does **not** predict success | **Do not reject candidates just for being anxious or stressed.** It is not a fair filter. |

> **Key message:** data-driven HR decisions can be **fairer, clearer and more accurate**.

---

## 4. Companies and employers

| Finding | What to do |
|---|---|
| Big IT-services firms pay about **11% less** for the same role and experience | **Risk of losing talent** to better-paying firms. Review pay against the market. |
| A "Senior" title adds **14–36%** even at the same experience | **Offer clear, faster promotion paths.** This keeps people without large pay jumps. |
| Maths and storytelling drive junior performance | **Invest in in-house training** on statistics and data storytelling. |
| Experience matters far more than city | **Hire from smaller cities and offer remote work.** Talent is not limited to Bengaluru or Mumbai. |

> **Key message:** to **attract and keep** data talent, companies need fair pay, clear growth paths and the right training.

---

## 5. Society and policy makers

| Finding | What to do |
|---|---|
| Low-paying jobs often list only generic skills (SEO, BPO, outsourcing) | **Upskilling programmes** should move workers towards analytical and technical skills. |
| Statistics and communication are the biggest skill gaps | **Government and industry skilling schemes** should include statistics and data storytelling, not just software tools. |
| Most jobs are concentrated in a few big cities | **Encourage remote and smaller-city jobs** to spread opportunity more widely. |

> **Key message:** closing the **data-skills gap** can raise incomes and spread opportunity.

---

## One-line summary for each group

| Who | One-line takeaway |
|---|---|
| **Students** | Gain experience early, learn specialised skills, and aim for Data Scientist roles. |
| **Universities** | Teach statistics and storytelling, not just tools. |
| **HR teams** | Use clear skill scores for juniors and personality insights for seniors. |
| **Companies** | Pay fairly, promote clearly, and train in maths and communication. |
| **Society** | Upskill people towards analytical skills and spread jobs beyond big cities. |

---

## A word of caution

These results come from **sample data**, and two of the datasets come from **a single company**. They show **strong patterns, not proof of cause**. Before acting on them at scale, check them against more data.
